# Questions

Good onboarding questions ask for a specific case, not a category. People
answer "what features do you want" with a wish list. They answer "walk me
through Tuesday morning when a customer calls to change their order" with the
roles, data, states and edge cases the wish list left out.

Ask two or three at a time. Use the user's own words back to them. When an
answer is vague, ask for an example rather than a definition.

## Openers

- What is it, in a sentence or two, and what made you want to build it?
- Who's the first person you picture using it? What are they doing just before
  they open it?
- If this worked perfectly six months from now, what would be different?
- Is anything like this out there already? What's wrong with it, for you?

## The main job

- Walk me through the most important thing someone does in it, from opening it
  to being done. What do they see first? What do they tap or type? What happens
  next?
- What's the moment they'd say "that's why I use this"?
- How often do they do this: every day, once a month, once?
- What are they using right now instead? Paper, a spreadsheet, a group chat,
  nothing?

## People and roles

- Who else touches it besides that first user? Someone who sets it up, someone
  who gets invited, someone who only looks, you doing support?
- What can each of them see that the others can't? What can each of them change?
- Can anyone use it without an account? Should they be able to?
- What happens when someone leaves a team, or an account needs deleting?

## Edges and states

- What does a brand new user see before there's any data?
- What happens when it fails halfway: no signal, a payment declined, a file too
  big?
- What's the biggest a list could realistically get? The longest a name could
  be?
- What should never happen, even by accident? (Double charges, a message to the
  wrong person, deleted work.)

## Data and content

- Where does the information on this screen come from? Who typed it, or where
  was it imported from?
- Who owns a record once it exists? Can it be shared, exported, deleted?
- Is any of it personal, medical, financial, or about kids or location?
- Does anything need to show up somewhere else: a calendar, an email, another
  app?

## Outside the app

- Does it need to tell people anything when they're not in it? What, and how
  would they switch that off?
- What other services does it depend on? Who has the accounts?
- Does it take money? From whom, how often, and what happens with refunds?

## Technical, in plain terms

Most of these the user will want you to propose. Propose, with a reason, and
ask for a yes.

- Anything you already use and want to stay on: a framework, a host, a
  database, an auth provider?
- Phones, desktop, or both? Does it have to work offline?
- How many people at launch, and how many in a year if it goes well?
- Where does it run, and what's the budget for running it?

## Challenging scope

Use these on each feature once the list exists. Ask them straight and accept
the answer.

- Who is worse off if this isn't in the first version?
- What's the simplest thing that would do for now? (A link instead of an
  account, a CSV instead of an integration, a manual step you do yourself.)
- These two pull against each other: <A> and <B>. Which wins in version one?
- This one is expensive because <reason>. Is it worth building before we know
  the core works?
- If you could only ship three of these, which three?

## Closing a topic

Before marking a topic covered, play it back in two or three sentences and ask
"is that right?" People correct summaries more readily than they volunteer
detail.

## For existing apps

- I've mapped what the app does today (show the map). What's wrong or missing?
- What do people complain about, or ask you for, most?
- What works well that we must not break?
- What's the change you'd make if you could only make one?
